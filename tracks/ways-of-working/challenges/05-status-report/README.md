# 05 · RAG status report

**Skills:** RAG (Red / Amber / Green) ratings against agreed rules, writing
for a busy reader, separating "for information" from "decision needed".

## Background
A **status report** tells the people funding a project whether it's on track,
in about 30 seconds. The heart of it is a **RAG** table: each area is rated
**Green** (on track), **Amber** (at risk, but there's a plan) or **Red**
(off track, needs help). Good status reports:

- Use **agreed rules** for the ratings, not gut feel. A report that's "Green,
  Green, Green, *surprise* Red" destroys trust.
- Back every Amber or Red with **evidence**: a date, a figure, a ticket.
- Make the **asks** explicit: which decisions are needed, from whom.
- Overall is never better than its worst area.

## Scenario
It's Friday 23 October 2026. Aisha has left you her raw notes in
`brief/status-notes-2026-10-23.md`, including Brightwell's RAG rules at the
bottom. Turn them into this week's report for Priya.

## Goal
Fill in every part of `status-report.md`:
1. The reporting period (`week ending 2026-10-23`) and your name.
2. The RAG table. Rate each area **by the rules in the notes** and justify
   each Amber/Red with the evidence.
3. A short **Summary** (15–80 words).
4. **Key risks and issues**, **Decisions needed** (what, and who decides) and
   **Next steps**, as bullet lists.
