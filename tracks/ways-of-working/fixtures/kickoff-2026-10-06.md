**Priya:** Before we go, Tom, how do we actually call TextBridge?

**Tom:** Three options. One: the order service calls the TextBridge API
directly and synchronously when an order ships. Simplest, but TextBridge
rate-limits us to **50 messages a second**. On a peak day we burst well past
that, and a slow vendor call would hold up the order service. Two: the order
service publishes an event to an **SQS queue**, and a new `beacon-worker`
consumes it and sends the texts at a steady rate, with retries. Three: a
nightly batch upload to TextBridge. That's no good for "out for delivery",
which has to arrive within minutes.

**Priya:** And you recommend?

**Tom:** Option two, the queue. It absorbs the peak bursts, respects the rate
limit, and retries don't block checkout. The cost is a new service to run, and
we'll need a dead-letter queue and alerting on it, or messages fail silently.
Texts may also arrive a few seconds later than today.

**Priya:** Agreed. Let's go with the queue. Tom, please write it up as an
architecture decision record by the 13th.

**Tom:** Will do. I'll lead the build of the TextBridge integration and I'm
accountable for it technically. Ben runs regression testing. Dan, can you be
consulted on the integration design?

**Dan:** Sure.

**Priya:** Great. Aisha, keep me informed on all of it. Thanks, everyone.
